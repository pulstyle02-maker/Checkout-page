THE EYE & ORBITAL CAVITY
BOUNDARIES & OPENINGS OF ORBITAL CAVITY
  • Orbital Margins (Base):
    • Superior: Frontal bone. Contains supraorbital notch/foramen (transmits supraorbital nerve & vessels).
    • Lateral: Processes of frontal and zygomatic bones.
    • Inferior: Zygomatic bone and maxilla.
    • Medial: Processes of maxilla and frontal bone.
  • Orbital Walls:
    • Roof (Superior): Orbital plate of frontal bone & lesser wing of sphenoid. Separates orbital cavity from anterior cranial fossa and frontal lobe.
    • Lateral Wall: Zygomatic bone & greater wing of sphenoid.
    • Floor (Inferior): Orbital plate of maxilla (separates from maxillary sinus), zygomatic bone, palatine bone. Contains infraorbital groove/canal.
    • Medial Wall: Frontal process of maxilla, lacrimal bone, orbital plate of ethmoid (separates from ethmoid sinuses), body of sphenoid. Contains anterior & posterior ethmoidal foramina.
  • Openings & Traversing Structures:
    • Orbital Opening: Anterior base. ~1/6th of eyeball protrudes through.
    • Optic Canal: Located in lesser wing of sphenoid at apex. Transmits Optic Nerve (CN II), meningeal sheaths, & Ophthalmic Artery.
    • Superior Orbital Fissure: Slit between greater/lesser wings of sphenoid. Transmits Oculomotor (CN III), Trochlear (CN IV), Abducens (CN VI), Ophthalmic (V1) branches (Lacrimal, Frontal, Nasociliary), & Superior Ophthalmic Vein.
    • Inferior Orbital Fissure: Between maxilla & greater wing of sphenoid. Transmits Maxillary Nerve (V2), its infraorbital/zygomatic branches, Inferior Ophthalmic Vein, & Sympathetic nerves.
    • Nasolacrimal Canal: Anterior medial wall in lacrimal bone. Transmits nasolacrimal duct (communicates with inferior meatus of nose).
    • Zygomaticotemporal/facial Foramina: Lateral wall (zygomatic bone). Transmit zygomaticotemporal & zygomaticofacial nerves (V2 branches).
    • Infraorbital Groove/Canal: Floor of orbit in maxilla. Transmits infraorbital nerve & vessels.

CRANIAL NERVES, GANGLIA & VASCULATURE OF THE EYE
  • Optic Nerve (CN II): Formed by ganglionic layer axons of retina. Surrounded by pia, arachnoid, dura mater. Subarachnoid space extends to eyeball (rise in CSF pressure transmits to back of eye → Papilledema). Decussation of medial half fibers at Optic Chiasma.
  • Trigeminal Nerve (CN V1 - Ophthalmic):
    • Lacrimal N: Runs on lat. rectus. Joins zygomaticotemporal n. (receives parasympathetics to lacrimal gland). Supplies skin of lat. upper lid/conjunctiva.
    • Frontal N: Runs on levator palpebrae. Divides into Supratrochlear & Supraorbital nerves (supply upper eyelid, forehead, scalp, frontal sinus).
    • Nasociliary N: Crosses optic n. Branches: Communicating to ciliary ganglion, Long ciliary (sympathetic to dilator pupillae & sensory to cornea), Post. ethmoidal (ethmoid/sphenoid sinuses), Infratrochlear (skin of eyelids/nose), Ant. ethmoidal (enters cranial cavity, then nasal cavity via slit alongside crista galli, terminates as ext. nasal n).
  • Ciliary Ganglion: Parasympathetic ganglion (pinhead size) in posterior orbit.
    • Parasympathetic Root: Preganglionic from CN III (Nerve to Inf. Oblique). Synapses. Postganglionic via Short Ciliary Nerves to sphincter pupillae/ciliary m.
    • Sympathetic Root: From internal carotid plexus. Passes through without synapsing.
    • Sensory Root: From eyeball via short ciliary nerves to nasociliary n.
  • Pterygopalatine Ganglion: Parasympathetic. Suspended from Maxillary Nerve (V2) in pterygopalatine fossa. Roots: Parasympathetic (Greater petrosal nerve/CN VII), Sympathetic (Deep petrosal nerve). Sends secretomotor fibers to lacrimal gland via zygomatic branch.
  • Ophthalmic Artery (Branch of ICA): Enters via optic canal.
    Branches: Central artery of retina (end artery, pierces optic n.) Muscular Ciliary (ant/post) Lacrimal Supratrochlear Supraorbital
    Anastomosis: Terminal branches (supratrochlear/supraorbital) anastomose with Facial A. (angular branch) at medial canthus.
  • Ophthalmic Veins: Superior (unites supraorbital/supratrochlear, communicates with facial v) & Inferior (communicates with pterygoid plexus). Both pass through superior orbital fissure and drain into Cavernous Sinus.
EYELIDS, CONJUNCTIVA & LACRIMAL APPARATUS
  • Eyelid Structure: Skin, subcutaneous tissue, orbicularis oculi, orbital septum (fibrous framework attached to margins), superior/inferior tarsal plates.
    • Glands: Sebaceous (Zeis) & Ciliary/modified sweat (Moll) open to lash follicles. Tarsal/Meibomian glands (long modified sebaceous) embedded in post. surface of tarsal plates, open behind lashes to secrete oily airtight seal preventing tear overflow.
    • Conjunctiva: Mucous membrane lining deep surface of lids, reflecting at sup/inf fornices onto anterior eyeball. Conjunctival sac is potential space. Subtarsal sulcus (groove on deep lid) traps foreign particles.
    • Medial Angle Features: Lacus lacrimalis (space), Caruncula lacrimalis (reddish-yellow elevation), Plica semilunaris (reddish semilunar fold), Papilla lacrimalis (elevation containing punctum lacrimale).
    • Ligaments: Lateral palpebral (connects tarsal plates to bony tubercle within margin), Medial palpebral (attaches plates to crest of lacrimal bone).
  • Lacrimal Apparatus Flow & Secretomotor Pathway:
    Lacrimal Gland (Orbital/Palpebral parts) → ~12 Ducts into Sup. Fornix → Cornea → Lacus Lacrimalis → Puncta Lacrimalis → Canaliculi → Lacrimal Sac → Nasolacrimal Duct (0.5 in. long) → Inferior Nasal Meatus (Lacrimal Fold prevents air backup)
    • Secretomotor Pathway: Lacrimal Nucleus (CN VII) → Nervus Intermedius → Greater Petrosal Nerve → Nerve of Pterygoid Canal → Pterygopalatine Ganglion (Synapse) → Maxillary N (V2) → Zygomatic N → Zygomaticotemporal N → Lacrimal N → Lacrimal Gland.

HNSS-A-001: Eye & Orbit (Clinicals & Coats)
ANATOMICAL STRUCTURE OF THE EYEBALL (3 COATS)
  • 1. External Fibrous Coat (Protective):
    • Sclera: Posterior opaque white part. Dense fibrous tissue. Pierced by optic nerve at lamina cribrosa (dural sheath fuses here), and by ciliary arteries/nerves & venae vorticosae.
    • Cornea: Anterior transparent part. Most important refractive medium (due to refractive index diff from air). Avascular (nourished by diffusion from aqueous humor & capillaries at edge). Innervated by long ciliary nerves (V1). Corneoscleral junction = Limbus.
  • 2. Middle Vascular Pigmented Coat (Uvea):
    • Choroid: Outer pigmented layer, inner highly vascular layer.
    • Ciliary Body: Ciliary ring (striae), Ciliary processes (radially arranged folds, give attachment to suspensory ligaments of lens, secrete aqueous humor), Ciliary muscle (meridianal & circular smooth muscle for accommodation).
    • Iris: Contractile pigmented diaphragm. Central aperture = pupil. Suspended in aqueous humor between cornea and lens. Divides space into anterior/posterior chambers. Contains sphincter & dilator pupillae.
  • 3. Inner Nervous Coat (Retina):
    • Outer pigmented layer & inner nervous layer. Anterior wavy edge where nervous tissue ends = Ora serrata.
    • Macula Lutea: Oval yellowish area at posterior pole. Central depression = Fovea centralis (area of most distinct vision).
    • Optic Disc (Blind Spot): 3mm medial to macula. Optic nerve exits here. Complete absence of rods/cones (insensitive to light). Central artery of retina pierces center. Pale pink on ophthalmoscopy.
  • Refractive Media:
    • Aqueous Humor: Clear fluid. Secreted by ciliary processes into posterior chamber → flows through pupil to anterior chamber → drains via spaces at iridocorneal angle into Canal of Schlemm. Exerts internal pressure to maintain optical shape. Blockage = Glaucoma (rise in intraocular pressure causing retinal degeneration/blindness).
    • Vitreous Body: Transparent gel filling eyeball behind lens. Contains hyaloid canal (remnant of fetal hyaloid a.). Supports retina and contributes slightly to magnification.
    • Lens: Biconvex, elastic capsule. Anterior cuboidal epithelium, equatorial lens fibers. Kept flattened by suspensory ligaments for distant vision. Presbyopia: Age-related loss of elasticity, lessening accommodation ability.
  • Eyeball Suspensions: Fascial sheath (bulbar fascia) surrounds eyeball, separating it from orbital fat. Tendons perforate sheath. Medial & Lateral check ligaments attach to orbital walls. Lower part thickened to form Suspensory ligament of the eye (hammock).
CLINICAL CORRELATES OF THE EYE & ORBIT
  • Horner's Syndrome: Interruption of sympathetic nerve supply to head (e.g., brainstem lesion, cervical sympathetic trunk trauma, stellate ganglion traction by cervical rib, apical lung tumor).
    • Miosis: Constricted pupil (unopposed sphincter pupillae due to paralyzed dilator).
    • Ptosis: Drooping upper eyelid (paralyzed superior tarsal muscle).
    • Anhidrosis: Lack of sweating & vasodilation on affected side of face.
    • Enophthalmos: Apparent depression of eyeball into orbital cavity.
  • Blowout Fractures of Maxilla: Blunt force (e.g., baseball) to orbit causes orbital fat to explode downward through orbital floor into maxillary sinus.
    • Causes displacement of eyeball → Double vision (Diplopia).
    • Entrapment of inferior rectus muscle → limits upward gaze.
    • Damage to infraorbital nerve → anesthesia/paresthesia of cheek skin and upper gum.
  • Clinical Testing of Recti & Oblique Muscles:
    • Origins of Sup/Inf recti are 23° medial to insertion. To test their pure action, align visual axis with muscle axis: Ask patient to abduct eye (look laterally), then look up (tests Sup Rectus) or down (tests Inf Rectus).
    • Pulley of Sup Oblique & origin of Inf Oblique are medial/anterior to insertions. Ask patient to adduct eye (look medially), then look down (tests Sup Oblique) or up (tests Inf Oblique).
    • Lat/Med recti tested simply by looking directly lateral or medial.
  • Strabismus: Nonparalytic (concomitant) caused by muscle imbalance (common in infancy). Paralytic caused by nerve lesions.
    • CN III Paralysis: Eye looks down & out (ext strabismus), severe ptosis, dilated pupil, no accommodation.
    • CN IV Paralysis: Diplopia when looking straight downward (patient cannot walk down stairs).
    • CN VI Paralysis: Eye pulled medially (internal strabismus). Cannot abduct.
  • Pupillary Reflexes:
    • Light Reflex (Direct/Consensual): Retina → Optic N → Optic Tract → Pretectal nuclei → Bilateral Edinger-Westphal nuclei (parasympathetic) → CN III → Ciliary Ganglion → Short Ciliary N → Sphincter pupillae.
    • Accommodation Reflex: Focusing on near object. Retina → Optic path → Visual cortex → Frontal eye field → Edinger-Westphal nucleus → CN III → Ciliary Ganglion → Sphincter pupillae (constricts pupil to reduce spherical aberration) & Ciliary muscle (convex lens). Medial recti contract for eye convergence.
  • Visual Fields: Hemianopia (blindness in half of visual field). Homonymous hemianopia (lesions of optic tract/radiation). Bitemporal hemianopia (pituitary tumor compressing optic chiasma).

HNSS-A-002: Nose & Paranasal Sinuses
NOSE & PARANASAL SINUSES
NASAL CAVITY BOUNDARIES
  • Roof (Apex): Narrow. Formed anteriorly by nasal and frontal bones (beneath bridge), in the middle by the cribriform plate of the ethmoid (beneath anterior cranial fossa), and posteriorly by the downward sloping body of the sphenoid.
  • Floor (Base): Formed by the superior surface of the hard palate (palatine process of maxilla and horizontal plate of palatine bone).
  • Medial Wall (Nasal Septum): Largely formed by the vomer (lower part) and the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid (upper part). The septal cartilage forms the anterior part. (Rarely situated exactly in the midline).
  • Lateral Wall: Possesses three projections of bone: the superior, middle, and inferior nasal conchae (shelves). Superior/middle are parts of ethmoid bone; inferior is a separate bone. The space below each concha is a meatus.
MEATUSES & PARANASAL SINUS DRAINAGE

Paranasal sinuses are air-filled cavities in maxilla, frontal, sphenoid, ethmoid bones lined with mucoperiosteum. Function: decrease skull weight (7-20%) and add voice resonance. Maxillary/sphenoid rudimentary at birth; expand significantly after 8th year.

NEUROVASCULAR SUPPLY OF NASAL CAVITY
  • Olfactory Nerves (CN I): Arise from olfactory receptor cells in olfactory mucous membrane (situated in upper part of nasal cavity above superior concha). Axons pass through cribriform plate to olfactory bulb.
    • Lesions: Fractures of anterior cranial fossa or frontal lobe tumors can damage nerves -> Anosmia (loss of smell). Meningitis can spread via olfactory nerve sheaths through cribriform plate.
  • Nerves of General Sensation: Branches of Ophthalmic (V1) and Maxillary (V2) nerves.
    • V1 Branches: Anterior ethmoidal nerve (descends through slit alongside crista galli, gives internal nasal branches, appears on face as external nasal branch).
    • V2 Branches: Nasopalatine nerve, Greater/Lesser palatine nerves.
  • Blood Supply: Arterial supply from branches of the maxillary artery (terminal ECA branch).
    • Sphenopalatine artery: Most important branch.
    • Anastomosis: Anastomoses with septal branch of superior labial artery (facial artery) in vestibule region.
    • Venous: Large submucous venous plexus in respiratory region warms inspired air.
  • Lymph Drainage: Vestibule drains to submandibular nodes. Remainder drains to upper deep cervical nodes.
  • Clinical - Epistaxis: Chronic profuse bleeding from nose. Common at anteroinferior portion of septum involving septal branches of sphenopalatine and facial vessels. Caused by nose picking or cocaine use.
  • Clinical - Transnasal Surgery: Convenient path to pituitary gland across nasal cavity to posterior roof, piercing sphenoid sinus to enter sella turcica from below.
EXTERNAL NOSE ANATOMY
  • Skeletal Framework: Nasal bones, frontal processes of maxillae, nasal part of frontal bone. Below bones: upper and lower plates of hyaline cartilage, small cartilages of ala nasi. Nasal septum (vomer, ethmoid, septal cartilage).
  • Muscles: Nasalis (compressor naris) & Depressor septi (sphincters). Dilator naris & Levator labii superioris alaeque nasi (dilators). All supplied by Facial Nerve (CN VII).
  • Blood Supply: Branches of ophthalmic and maxillary arteries. Branches from facial artery supply skin of ala and lower septum.
  • Sensory Innervation: Infratrochlear & External nasal branches of Ophthalmic nerve (V1) innervate bridge/crest to tip. Infraorbital branch of Maxillary nerve (V2) supplies side.

HNSS-A-003: Ear Anatomy (External, Middle, Internal)
EAR ANATOMY & PATHWAYS
EXTERNAL EAR ANATOMY & NEUROVASCULATURE
  • Auricle (Pinna): Thin plate of elastic cartilage covered by skin.
    • Components: Helix (elevated margin), Tragus (projection extending over meatus), Lobule (earlobe, NO cartilage), Concha (deepest depression leading into meatus).
    • Sensory Nerves: Lesser occipital (C2) & Great auricular (C2, C3) are main sensory nerves. Supplemented by small components of facial (CN VII) & glossopharyngeal (CN IX). Auriculotemporal (V3) & auricular branch of vagus (CN X) supply external surface of tympanic membrane & external meatus.
    • Lymph Drainage: Superficial parotid, mastoid, and superficial cervical lymph nodes.
  • External Auditory (Acoustic) Meatus: Slender curved tube conducting sound waves. Outer 1/3 elastic cartilage, Inner 2/3 bony (tympanic plate of temporal bone). Skin lined. Outer third has hairs, sebaceous, and ceruminous glands (secrete cerumen/earwax for sticky barrier).
  • Tympanic Membrane (Eardrum): Thin, fibrous, oblique interface facing inferiorly/anteriorly/laterally. Slightly concave laterally.
    • Features: Umbo (small depression produced by tip of handle of malleus), Cone of light (radiates ant/inf from umbo), Pars flaccida (slack triangular area bounded by ant/post malleolar folds), Pars tensa (tense remainder).
    • Nerves: Extremely sensitive to pain. Outer surface: Auriculotemporal (V3) & auricular branch of vagus. Inner aspect: Tympanic plexus of glossopharyngeal (CN IX).
    • Clinical: Perforation (ruptured eardrum) via otitis media, foreign object, pressure → conduction deafness. Examine by pulling auricle upward/backward in adult.
MIDDLE EAR (TYMPANIC CAVITY) BOUNDARIES & CONTENTS
  • Structure: Air-containing cavity in petrous temporal bone. Two parts: Tympanic cavity proper (medial to eardrum) & Epitympanic recess (superior). Six-sided box.
    • Roof (Superior): Tegmen tympani (thin bone). Separates from middle cranial fossa/temporal lobe. (Fracture = CSF leak into tympanic cavity).
    • Floor (Jugular wall): Thin bone. Separates from superior bulb of Internal Jugular Vein. Pierced by tympanic nerve (branch of CN IX).
    • Anterior (Carotid wall): Upper opening for Tensor tympani muscle. Lower opening for Auditory tube. Separates from Internal Carotid Artery (in carotid canal).
    • Posterior (Mastoid wall): Upper opening: Aditus ad antrum (entrance to mastoid antrum). Pyramid (conical projection housing stapedius muscle, tendon emerges from apex). Prominence of facial canal (facial nerve descends here). Opening for chorda tympani nerve.
    • Medial (Labyrinthine wall): Separates from internal ear. Promontory (rounded projection from 1st turn of cochlea). Fenestra vestibuli (Oval window, above/behind promontory, closed by stapes footplate/annular ligament). Fenestra cochleae (Round window, below post. promontory, closed by secondary tympanic membrane). Prominence of facial canal & lateral semicircular canal. Processus cochleariformis (pulley for tensor tympani tendon).
    • Lateral (Membranous wall): Tympanic membrane & epitympanic recess.
  • Auditory Ossicles (Synovial joints):
    • Malleus: Largest. Head articulates with incus in epitympanic recess. Neck. Handle attached to eardrum. Ant process to ant wall via ligament. Lat process to malleolar folds.
    • Incus: Body articulates with malleus head. Long process bends medially to articulate with stapes head. Short process to post wall via ligament.
    • Stapes: Head to incus. Neck receives stapedius muscle. 2 limbs diverge to oval base. Base (footplate) attaches to oval window margin via annular ligament.
    • Mechanics: Eardrum moves medially → Malleus handle medially → Head/Incus body laterally → Incus long process/Stapes medially into oval window → Perilymph motion → Round window bulges. Leverage increases 1.3 to 1, area 17x, total pressure 22x.
  • Middle Ear Muscles:
    • Tensor tympani: Inserts on malleus handle. Dampens loud sound. Innervated by Mandibular Nerve (V3).
    • Stapedius: Smallest skeletal muscle. Inserts on stapes neck. Dampens loud sound. Innervated by Facial Nerve (CN VII). Paralysis = Hyperacusis.
  • Auditory Tube: Connects ant wall to nasopharynx. Equalizes pressure, drains secretions. Lateral 1/3 bony, medial 2/3 cartilaginous. Opened actively by Tensor veli palatini & Levator veli palatini during swallowing/yawning. Route for infection spread.
  • Mastoid Area: Antrum & honeycomb air cells. Aditus connects epitympanic recess to antrum. Antrum Relations: Ant (tympanic cavity/aditus), Post (sigmoid venous sinus, cerebellum), Lat (suprameatal triangle 1.5cm thick), Med (post. semicircular canal), Sup (tegmen tympani/temporal lobe), Inf (mastoid air cells). Not present at birth (develops 2nd year via SCM pull).

HNSS-A-003: MIDDLE EAR NERVES, INTERNAL EAR & PATHWAYS
  • Middle Ear Nerves & Communications:
    • Facial Nerve (CN VII): Enters facial canal at bottom of int. acoustic meatus. Expands to form sensory Geniculate ganglion at medial wall. Bends sharply backward above promontory, curves down on post wall behind pyramid, exits stylomastoid foramen. Branches in petrous part: 1) Greater petrosal nerve (preganglionic parasympathetics to pterygopalatine ganglion). 2) Nerve to stapedius. 3) Chorda tympani (taste ant 2/3 tongue, preganglionic parasympathetics to submandibular ganglion; exits petrotympanic fissure).
    • Glossopharyngeal Nerve (CN IX): Tympanic nerve arises below jugular foramen, pierces floor to promontory, forms Tympanic plexus. Supplies middle ear lining. Gives off Lesser petrosal nerve (secretomotor to parotid via otic ganglion).
    • Caroticotympanic nerve: Sympathetic from carotid plexus, joins tympanic plexus.
  • Clinical Infections: Otitis Media: Infection ascends via auditory tube. Causes bulging red eardrum. Complications: Spread to mastoid (acute mastoiditis), brain/meninges (meningitis/cerebral abscess), facial nerve (palsy), inner ear (labyrinthitis/vertigo), sigmoid sinus (thrombosis). Otosclerosis: Abnormal bone fixes stapes footplate = conduction deafness.
  • Internal Ear (Labyrinth): Medial to middle ear in petrous temporal bone.
    • Bony Labyrinth (Contains Perilymph): 1) Vestibule (central, contains saccule/utricle). 2) 3 Semicircular canals (superior, posterior, lateral - at right angles, each has ampulla, open to vestibule). 3) Cochlea (snail shell, opens to ant vestibule, central pillar = modiolus, spiral lamina, divided into scala vestibuli/tympani by basilar membrane).
    • Membranous Labyrinth (Contains Endolymph): 1) Utricle & Saccule (in vestibule, sensory receptors for gravity/static orientation). 2) 3 Semicircular ducts (in canals, ampullae receptors detect dynamic acceleration/deceleration). 3) Duct of cochlea (in bony cochlea, connected to saccule via ductus reuniens, contains Spiral organ of Corti on basilar membrane).
  • Vestibulocochlear Nerve (CN VIII) & Pathway: Exits via internal acoustic meatus.
    • Vestibular fibers: Central processes of nerve cells in vestibular ganglion in int. acoustic meatus. Originate from vestibule/semicircular canals. Concerned with equilibrium/head movement. Lesions = vertigo, nystagmus.
    • Cochlear fibers: Central processes of nerve cells in spiral ganglion (in modiolus). Originate in Spiral organ of Corti. Concerned with hearing. Lesions = deafness, tinnitus.

HNSS-A-004: TONGUE NEUROVASCULATURE, LESIONS & CARCINOMA
  • Tongue Anatomy: Mass of striated muscle. Divided by V-shaped sulcus terminalis into Ant 2/3 (oral part/body) & Post 1/3 (pharyngeal part/root). Apex of sulcus = foramen cecum (remnant of thyroglossal duct).
    • Papillae: Filiform, fungiform, vallate (large, alongside sulcus terminalis on ant 2/3). Post 1/3 has no papillae, irregular surface due to underlying Lingual Tonsil.
    • Ventral surface: Lingual frenulum, deep lingual vein visible laterally, plica fimbriata.
  • Tongue Muscles (Intrinsic/Extrinsic): Divided by median fibrous septum.

    • Blood Supply: Lingual artery, Tonsillar branch of facial artery, Ascending pharyngeal artery. Veins drain to IJV.

    • CN XII Lesion (Motor): Muscle atrophy/wrinkling on affected side. On protrusion, tongue deviates TOWARD the paralyzed side (due to unopposed action of normal contralateral genioglossus pushing its side forward).

    • Lymph Drainage (Lingual Carcinoma spread): Tip → Submental nodes. Sides of ant 2/3 → Submandibular & Deep cervical. Post 1/3 → Deep cervical nodes (Jugulodigastric & Jugulo-omohyoid nodes).

SKULL & SCALP
HNSS-A-005: SKULL NORMAS, FOSSAE, SUTURES & FONTANELLES
  • Skull Normas:
    • Frontalis (Anterior): Frontal bone (superciliary arches, supraorbital notch, frontal air sinuses), Nasal bones (bridge), Maxillae (infraorbital foramen, alveolar arch, maxillary sinus), Zygomatic bone (zygomaticofacial/temporal foramina), Mandible.
    • Verticalis (Superior): Frontal bone & two parietal bones. Coronal & Sagittal sutures. Metopic suture if frontal halves fail to fuse.
    • Occipitalis (Posterior): Parietal bones, Squamous occipital bone. Lambdoid suture. External occipital protuberance (ligamentum nuchae attachment), Superior nuchal lines.

    • Lateralis (Lateral): Frontal, Parietal, Occipital, Temporal, Sphenoid greater wing. Includes Temporal, Infratemporal & Pterygopalatine fossae.

    • Temporal Fossa: Boundaries: temporal lines (sup/inf), frontal process of zygomatic (ant), post root of zygomatic arch (post), infratemporal crest (inf), zygomatic arch (lat). Contents: Temporalis m., superficial temporal a. Communicates with infratemporal fossa deep to zygomatic arch.
    • Infratemporal Fossa: Boundaries: maxilla (ant), styloid/spine sphenoid (post), infratemporal crest (sup), alveolar border (inf), lat pterygoid plate (med), ramus mandible (lat). Contents: Med/Lat pterygoid muscles, Maxillary artery, Pterygoid venous plexus, Mandibular n. (V3), chorda tympani, otic ganglion. Communicates with pterygopalatine fossa via pterygomaxillary fissure.
    • Pterygopalatine Fossa: Pyramidal space behind orbit. Boundaries: maxilla (ant), pterygoid process (post), orbit apex (sup), perpendicular plate palatine bone (med), pterygomaxillary fissure (lat). Contents: Maxillary n. (V2), nerve of pterygoid canal, Pterygopalatine ganglion, terminal maxillary artery. Communicates with: orbit (inf. orbital fissure), nasal cavity (sphenopalatine foramen), cranial cavity (foramen rotundum), oral cavity (pterygopalatine canal), ext base of skull (pterygoid canal), nasopharynx (pharyngeal canal).
  • Sutures, Fontanelles & Age Changes:
    • Sutures: Connect bones via sutural ligaments. Ossify in middle age.
    • Anterior (Bregmatic) Fontanelle: Diamond-shaped (between frontal/parietal). Closes at 18 months. Clinical: Palpation assesses hydration (depressed = dehydration) & ICP (bulging = raised ICP). Site for passing needle to subarachnoid space/lateral ventricle.
    • Posterior (Lambdoid) Fontanelle: Triangular (between parietal/occipital). Closes by 1 year. Sphenoidal/Mastoidal fontanelles on sides.
    • Neonatal Skull: Face to cranium ratio 1:8 (Adult 1:1). Bones smooth/unilaminar (no diploe). Tympanic part is a C-ring (membrane faces inferiorly, external meatus mostly cartilaginous). Mastoid process absent (forceps delivery can damage exposed facial nerve!).
  • Clinical Skull Fractures: Adult inner table brittle. "Pond" fracture in resilient infant skull. Pterion: Thinnest lateral wall (frontal/parietal/temporal/sphenoid junction) overlies anterior division of middle meningeal artery -> epidural hemorrhage. Base fractures: Ant fossa (cribriform plate damage -> CSF rhinorrhea, epistaxis, subconjunctival hemorrhage), Mid fossa (weakest part, petrous temporal damage -> CN VII/VIII injury, CSF/blood from external meatus), Post fossa (jugular foramen damage -> CN IX, X, XI injury, blood tracking to nape of neck).

HNSS-A-007: MUSCLES OF FACIAL EXPRESSION & MASTICATION
  • Muscles of Facial Expression: Develop from 2nd Pharyngeal Arch. Innervated entirely by Facial Nerve (CN VII). Insert into skin/fascia (no deep fascia in face). Regulate facial orifices (accessory muscles of vision, olfaction, respiration, feeding, speech).
    • Eyelids: Orbicularis oculi (sphincter, closes lids), Occipitofrontalis (dilator, raises eyebrows/wrinkles forehead), Corrugator supercilii.
    • Nostrils: Nasalis (compressor naris), Depressor septi (sphincters). Dilator naris, Levator labii superioris alaeque nasi (dilators).
    • Lips/Cheeks: Orbicularis oris (primary sphincter). Buccinator (muscle of cheek, compresses against teeth to position food, pierced by parotid duct, decussates at angle). Zygomaticus major/minor, Levator anguli oris, Risorius, Depressor anguli oris, Depressor labii inferioris, Mentalis.
    • Modiolus: A fibromuscular chiasma (node) situated lateral to the angle of the mouth where several facial muscles converge and decussate.
  • Facial Nerve Paralysis (Bell's Palsy): Lower motor neuron lesion. Causes mask-like expressionless face. Inability to close eyelids, drooping lower lid, sagging angle of mouth, dribbling saliva, food accumulates in cheek (buccinator paralysis).
  • Muscles of Mastication: Develop from 1st Pharyngeal Arch. Innervated by Mandibular Nerve (V3).
    • Temporalis, Masseter, Lateral Pterygoid, Medial Pterygoid. (Details of attachments/actions covered in TMJ section).
HNSS-A-008: NEUROVASCULAR SUPPLY OF FACE
  • Sensory Innervation (Trigeminal Nerve): Little overlap compared to trunk dermatomes. Proprioceptive fibers of facial muscles also travel via trigeminal.
    • Ophthalmic (V1): Supratrochlear, Supraorbital, Infratrochlear, External nasal, Lacrimal.
    • Maxillary (V2): Infraorbital, Zygomaticofacial, Zygomaticotemporal.
    • Mandibular (V3): Mental, Buccal, Auriculotemporal.
    • Exception: Skin over angle of mandible & parotid gland is supplied by Great Auricular Nerve (C2, C3) from cervical plexus.
    • Clinical: Trigeminal Neuralgia (Tic douloureux) - excruciating pain, usually V2 or V3 distribution.
  • Motor Innervation (Facial Nerve CN VII): Exits stylomastoid foramen, enters parotid gland (between superficial/deep lobes), divides into 5 terminal branches: Temporal, Zygomatic, Buccal (supplies buccinator & upper lip/nostril), Mandibular, Cervical (supplies platysma, depressor anguli oris).
  • Arterial Supply: Very rich supply (skin flaps rarely necrose).
    • Facial Artery (from ECA): Arches over submandibular gland, curves around inf margin of mandible (pulse point) ant to masseter. Tortuous course to angle of mouth, ascends side of nose. Branches: Submental, Inf labial, Sup labial, Lateral nasal, Angular (terminal branch).
    • Superficial Temporal Artery (from ECA): Ascends over zygomatic arch (pulse point). Branches: Transverse facial (runs above parotid duct), Ant (frontal) & Post (parietal) branches.
    • Small Arteries: Supraorbital, supratrochlear, infraorbital, mental (accompany sensory nerves).
    • Crucial Anastomosis: Angular artery (Facial a. / ECA) anastomoses with terminal branches of Ophthalmic a. (Supratrochlear/Supraorbital / ICA) at the medial canthus of the eye. Important for collateral circulation.
  • Venous Drainage:
    • Facial Vein: Formed at medial angle by supraorbital + supratrochlear. Descends behind facial artery. Crosses mandible, joined by ant div of retromandibular vein, drains to IJV. Communicates with pterygoid plexus via deep facial vein.
  • Lymphatic Drainage: Forehead/ant face -> Submandibular nodes. Lat face/eyelids -> Parotid nodes. Central lower lip/chin -> Submental nodes.
HNSS-A-009: DANGER AREA OF THE FACE
  • Boundaries: Triangular area bounded by the nose, the medial angle of the eye, and the upper lip.
  • Anatomical Justification & Clinical Spread:
    • The facial vein and its connecting superior ophthalmic veins are valveless.
    • The facial vein communicates directly with the Cavernous Sinus (inside the cranium) via the Superior Ophthalmic Vein.
    • A boil, pimple, or infection in this danger area can cause retrograde thrombosis of the facial vein.
    • Organisms spread through the superior ophthalmic veins into the cavernous sinus, causing Cavernous Sinus Thrombosis, which can be rapidly fatal (affects CN III, IV, V1, V2, VI running in/near sinus).

HNSS-A-010: MANDIBLE & TEMPOROMANDIBULAR JOINT (TMJ)
  • Mandible Bony Features: Largest/strongest face bone. Horseshoe-shaped body & two rami.
    • Body: Symphysis menti (fusion line), Mental foramen (transmits terminal inf alveolar n/v), Mental spines / genial tubercles (medial surface, attach genioglossus/geniohyoid), Mylohyoid line, Submandibular/Sublingual fossae, Digastric fossa, Alveolar part (16 tooth sockets).
    • Ramus: Coronoid process (anterior, Temporalis attachment), Condyloid process / Head (posterior, TMJ articulation), Mandibular notch.
    • Medial Ramus: Mandibular foramen (transmits Inf alveolar n/v into mandibular canal), Lingula (anterior projection for sphenomandibular ligament attachment).
    • Fractures: Force transmits around the bony ring; frequently causes multiple/bilateral fractures far from impact. Strong mastication muscles cause severe displacement.
  • TMJ Classification & Structures: Synovial joint (modified hinge) between articular tubercle/mandibular fossa (temporal bone) and condyloid process (mandible). Capsule surrounds joint.
    • Articular Disc: Fibrocartilage plate dividing joint into upper/lower cavities. Upper surface concavoconvex, lower surface concave. Attached to capsule & lat. pterygoid tendon.
    • Ligaments: 1) Lateral temporomandibular: Strengthens lateral capsule, limits posterior movement (protects external auditory meatus from fracture). 2) Sphenomandibular: Spine of sphenoid to lingula (remnant of 1st pharyngeal arch). 3) Stylomandibular: Styloid process to angle of mandible (thickened deep cervical fascia).
  • TMJ Movements, Mechanics & Muscles:
    • Depression (Opening): Head rotates on lower surface of disc (horizontal axis). Then Lateral Pterygoid pulls neck & disc forward onto articular tubercle. Digastric, geniohyoid, mylohyoid assist.
    • Elevation (Closing): Head/disc move backward, then head rotates on disc. Temporalis, Masseter, Medial Pterygoids.
    • Protrusion: Articular disc pulled forward onto articular tubercle. Both Lateral Pterygoids (+ med pterygoids). Movement entirely in upper cavity.
    • Retraction: Disc/head pulled backward into fossa. Posterior fibers of Temporalis.
    • Lateral Chewing: Alternate protrusion/retraction on each side + rotation.
  • TMJ Relations & Clinicals: Ant (mandibular notch, masseteric n/a), Post (tympanic plate, glenoid process of parotid), Lat (parotid, fascia, skin), Med (maxillary a/v, auriculotemporal n). Dislocation: Occurs during depression (yawning). Head/disc move anterior to summit of articular tubercle into unstable position. Spasm of lat. pterygoids locks jaw open. Reduced by pressing downward & backward on lower molars.
HNSS-A-011: OTIC & SUBMANDIBULAR GANGLIA
  • Otic Ganglion: Parasympathetic ganglion (pinhead size).
    • Location: Infratemporal fossa, medial to Mandibular N (V3), adherent to nerve to medial pterygoid.
    • Roots: Preganglionic from Glossopharyngeal (CN IX) via tympanic nerve → tympanic plexus → Lesser petrosal nerve (passes through foramen ovale).
    • Distribution: Postganglionic secretomotor fibers join Auriculotemporal N. (branch of V3) to supply the Parotid salivary gland.
  • Submandibular Ganglion: Parasympathetic ganglion.
    • Location: Deep to submandibular gland, suspended from Lingual N. (branch of V3).
    • Roots: Preganglionic from Facial N. (CN VII) via Chorda tympani (exits petrotympanic fissure), which joins the Lingual N in the infratemporal fossa.
    • Distribution: Postganglionic secretomotor fibers pass directly to Submandibular & Sublingual salivary glands.
HNSS-A-012: HYOID BONE
  • Anatomical Features: Mobile, U-shaped bone in midline of neck below mandible. Does NOT articulate with any other bone. Forms base for tongue.
    • Parts: Body, two greater cornua, two lesser cornua.
    • Attachments: Suspended by stylohyoid ligament (from styloid process) and thyrohyoid membrane (from thyroid cartilage). Base for tongue (hyoglossus, genioglossus). Attachment for suprahyoid (digastric, stylohyoid, mylohyoid, geniohyoid) and infrahyoid (sternohyoid, omohyoid, thyrohyoid) muscles. Middle pharyngeal constrictor attaches to greater cornu.

HNSS-A-013: CERVICAL VERTEBRAE
  • Typical Cervical Vertebrae (C3-C6): Small body, large triangular vertebral foramen, bifid spinous process. Transverse processes possess a foramen transversarium (transmits vertebral artery & veins, except C7 which transmits only the vertebral vein). Superior/inferior articular processes have facets covered by hyaline cartilage forming 4 synovial joints per vertebra. Superior/inferior notches form intervertebral foramina for spinal nerves.
  • Atypical Cervical Vertebrae:
    • C1 (Atlas): No body, no spinous process. Anterior & posterior arches, lateral masses (superior articular facets articulate with occipital condyles).
    • C2 (Axis): Peg-like odontoid process (dens) projects upward from superior surface of body (represents fused body of atlas).
    • C7 (Vertebra Prominens): Longest spinous process, non-bifid. Small foramen transversarium (vertebral vein only).
  • Joints of Cervical Vertebrae:
    • Atlanto-Occipital: Synovial. Occipital condyles + superior facets of lateral masses of atlas. Allows flexion/extension ("Yes" movement). Ligaments: Ant/Post atlanto-occipital membranes.
    • Atlantoaxial: 3 Synovial joints (1 median: dens + ant arch of atlas; 2 lateral: lateral masses). Allows rotation ("No" movement). Ligaments: Apical (apex of dens to foramen magnum), Alar (dens to occipital condyles), Cruciate (transverse ligament binds dens to ant arch; vertical part from axis body to foramen magnum), Membrana tectoria (upward continuation of post longitudinal ligament).
    • Intervertebral Discs: Fibrocartilage (anulus fibrosus + nucleus pulposus). Absent between skull/C1 and C1/C2. Act as semielastic shock absorbers. Uncovertebral joints may be present laterally in lower cervical region.
HNSS-A-014: PREVERTEBRAL MUSCLES

    • Muscles: Longus colli, Longus capitis, Rectus capitis anterior, Rectus capitis lateralis.
    • Attachments: Transverse processes/bodies of cervical vertebrae up to the occipital bone.
    • Actions: Flexion of head and neck, lateral flexion.
    • Innervation: Anterior rami of cervical nerves.

HNSS-A-016: FACIAL SPACES & INFECTION SPREAD
  • Retropharyngeal Space: Potential space between buccopharyngeal fascia (covering pharynx/esophagus) and prevertebral fascia.
    • Clinical Spread: Blood, pus, or air can track directly downward into the Superior Mediastinum of the thorax. Example: Tuberculous osteomyelitis of cervical vertebrae destroys bone, pus limited by prevertebral fascia, bulges into pharynx, tracks down or laterally to posterior triangle (points behind SCM).
  • Submandibular Space: Below mandible.
    • Clinical Spread: Dental infections (especially lower molars) spread medially causing Ludwig's Angina. Acute infection pushes tongue forward and upward, leading to edema of vocal cords and fatal airway obstruction.
  • Masticatory Space: Encloses muscles of mastication. Spread here causes severe trismus (locked jaw).
  • Visceral Space: Within pretracheal fascia. Infection spreads downward into the superior mediastinum.

HNSS-A-018: ANSA CERVICALIS
  • Location & Formation: Nerve loop located in the anterior triangle (carotid triangle), superficial to or within the carotid sheath.
    • Superior Root: Formed by descending branch of hypoglossal nerve (composed of C1 fibers that hitchhiked on CN XII).
    • Inferior Root: Formed by descending cervical nerve (C2, C3 from cervical plexus).

HNSS-A-019: STERNOCLEIDOMASTOID & TRAPEZIUS

    • Action: Elevates, retracts, and rotates scapula.
    • Clinical: CN XI crosses posterior triangle superficially. Laceration paralyzes trapezius -> dropped shoulder, inability to elevate arm above 90° (after initial abduction by deltoid).

HNSS-A-021: TRACHEA & ESOPHAGUS (CERVICAL PART)
  • Cervical Trachea: Mobile fibroelastic tube. C6 (below cricoid) to T4/T5 (carina). U-shaped hyaline cartilage rings kept patent, free posterior ends connected by trachealis smooth muscle. Lined with pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium.
    • Relations: Anterior (Skin, fascia, isthmus of thyroid over 2nd-4th rings, inf thyroid veins, jugular arch, thyroidea ima a., strap muscles). Lateral (Thyroid lobes, carotid sheath). Posterior (Esophagus, Right/Left Recurrent Laryngeal Nerves in tracheoesophageal groove).
    • Neurovascular: Blood: Inferior thyroid arteries (upper 2/3). Nerves: Vagus & recurrent laryngeal nerves. Lymph: Pretracheal/paratracheal/deep cervical.
    • Clinical: Tracheostomy (vertical incision through 2nd/3rd rings, retract isthmus. Danger to ant jugular veins, inf thyroid veins, thyroidea ima a., recurrent laryngeal nerves, pleura in kids). Cricothyroidotomy (through cricothyroid ligament).
  • Cervical Esophagus: Muscular tube (10 inches total length). Begins at C6. Descends posterior to trachea, anterior to prevertebral fascia. Inclines slightly to left. Blood supply: Inferior thyroid arteries.
HNSS-A-022: THYROID & PARATHYROID GLANDS
  • Thyroid Gland Anatomy: Two pear-shaped lobes connected by isthmus. Apices to oblique line of thyroid cartilage. Bases at 4th/5th tracheal ring. Pyramidal lobe often extends upward from isthmus (levator glandulae thyroideae). Enclosed in pretracheal fascia (moves upward on swallowing).
    • Relations: Anterolateral (Strap muscles, SCM). Posterolateral (Carotid sheath). Medial (Larynx, trachea, pharynx, esophagus, cricothyroid m., external/recurrent laryngeal nerves). Posterior (Parathyroid glands).
  • Vascular Supply & Crucial Surgical Nerve Relations:
    • Superior Thyroid Artery: Branch of ECA. Descends to upper pole. Closely related to External Laryngeal Nerve (supplies cricothyroid). Surgical damage = inability to tense vocal folds → weak, hoarse voice.
    • Inferior Thyroid Artery: Branch of thyrocervical trunk (subclavian). Ascends behind gland, turns medially. Closely related to Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve. Surgical damage = vocal fold paralysis.
    • Thyroidea Ima Artery: Inconstant (from brachiocephalic/aortic arch). Ascends in front of trachea to isthmus.
    • Veins: Superior/Middle (drain to IJV), Inferior (anastomose in front of trachea, drain to L. Brachiocephalic v.).
  • Parathyroid Glands: Usually 4 ovoid bodies on posterior border of thyroid within fascial capsule. Secrete PTH (increases blood Ca++). Blood supply: Inferior thyroid arteries.
    • Development: Inferior parathyroids (Parathyroid III) from 3rd pharyngeal pouch (with thymus, may migrate to superior mediastinum). Superior parathyroids (Parathyroid IV) from 4th pouch.
  • Clinical: Retrosternal goiter (downward expansion compresses trachea/veins since sternothyroid muscle limits upward expansion). Thyroglossal cyst/fistula (remnant of embryonic descent from foramen cecum). Lingual thyroid.
HNSS-A-023 & 024: CAROTID ARTERIES & CAROTID BODY
  • Common Carotid Artery (CCA): Right from brachiocephalic, Left from aortic arch. Ascends in carotid sheath. Bifurcates at C4 (upper border of thyroid cartilage). Palpate pulse at anterior border of SCM.
  • External Carotid Artery (ECA): Supplies neck, face, scalp, jaws.
    Branches: Sup. Thyroid → Ascending Pharyngeal → Lingual → Facial → Occipital → Post. Auricular → Terminal: Maxillary & Sup. Temporal.
    • Relations: Anterolateral (SCM, hypoglossal n, post digastric, stylohyoid, facial n. in parotid). Medial (pharynx wall, ICA, stylopharyngeus, glossopharyngeal n).
  • Internal Carotid Artery (ICA): No branches in neck. Enters carotid canal. Ascends in cavernous sinus. Branches in cranium: Ophthalmic, Ant/Mid Cerebral, Post Communicating (Circle of Willis). Supplies brain/eye. Atherosclerosis causes visual impairment/contralateral motor loss.
  • Carotid Sinus (A-024): Dilation at bifurcation (terminal CCA or origin ICA). Thin media, thick adventitia. Reflex pressoreceptor (baroreceptor) for BP regulation (slows heart, vasodilates). Innervated by Glossopharyngeal (CN IX). Hypersensitivity causes syncope.
  • Carotid Body (A-024): Small structure posterior to bifurcation. Chemoreceptor (sensitive to excess CO2, reduced O2). Innervated by CN IX (and X). Reflexly increases BP/HR/respiration.

HNSS-A-025: HEAD & NECK VENOUS SUPPLY
  • Internal Jugular Vein (IJV):
    • Continuation of Sigmoid sinus. Exits skull via jugular foramen.
    • Descends in carotid sheath (lateral to CCA/ICA and Vagus). Deep cervical nodes run alongside.
    • Tributaries: Inferior petrosal sinus, Facial, Pharyngeal, Lingual, Sup/Mid thyroid veins.
    • Ends by joining Subclavian vein to form Brachiocephalic vein behind medial clavicle. Has superior/inferior bulbs (bicuspid valve near inf bulb).
    • Clinical: Penetrating wounds risk air embolism (wall attached to deep fascia, cannot collapse). Catheterization via lesser supraclavicular fossa (ant/post approaches, aim toward opposite nipple).
  • External Jugular Vein (EJV):
    • Formed behind angle of mandible by: Posterior Auricular V. + Posterior division of Retromandibular V.
    • Descends obliquely across SCM, deep to platysma. Pierces investing fascia in posterior triangle, drains into Subclavian vein.
    • Tributaries: Post. ext jugular, Transverse cervical, Suprascapular, Anterior jugular.
    • Clinical: Serves as a venous manometer (JVP). Normally level is 1/3 up neck at 30° supine. Visible in right heart failure.
  • Anterior Jugular Vein: Descends close to midline. Joined to opposite side by jugular arch above sternum. Drains to EJV deep to SCM.
  • Subclavian Vein: Continuation of axillary vein at 1st rib. Joins IJV to form brachiocephalic. Receives EJV, thoracic duct (left), right lymphatic duct. Catheterization (infraclavicular/supraclavicular) risks pneumothorax, hemothorax, phrenic/brachial plexus injury.
HNSS-A-026: LYMPHATICS OF HEAD & NECK
  • Pericervical Collar Nodes: Superficial ring around head/neck junction.
    • Occipital: Drain back of scalp.
    • Mastoid: Drain scalp above ear, auricle, external meatus.
    • Parotid: Drain scalp above parotid, eyelids, auricle.
    • Submandibular: Drain ant scalp, nose, cheek, lips (except central lower), gums, ant 2/3 tongue, sinuses. (Commonly enlarged in dental infections).
    • Submental: Drain tip of tongue, central lower lip, chin.
  • Cervical Regional Nodes: Anterior & Superficial cervical (along EJV/AJV), Retropharyngeal, Laryngeal, Tracheal.
  • Deep Cervical Nodes (Terminal Group):
    • Form vertical chain along IJV in carotid sheath. Receive ALL lymph from head/neck.
    • Jugulodigastric Node: Drains tonsil and tongue (below/behind angle of jaw).
    • Jugulo-omohyoid Node: Drains tongue.
    • Efferent vessels form Jugular Trunks (Left drains to thoracic duct, Right to right lymphatic duct).
    • Clinical: Enlarged in metastatic carcinoma. Block Dissection: Removal en bloc of IJV, fascia, lymph nodes, submandibular gland (preserving carotids/vagus).
HNSS-A-027: CERVICAL PLEXUS

HNSS-A-028: PHARYNX ANATOMY & MUSCLES
  • Divisions: Nasopharynx (pharyngeal tonsil, auditory tube opening, torus tubarius, pharyngeal recess), Oropharynx (palatine tonsil, vallecula), Laryngopharynx (piriform fossa).
  • Pharyngeal Constrictor Muscles (Circular Layer):
    • Superior Constrictor: Attaches to pterygomandibular raphe.
    • Middle Constrictor: Attaches to hyoid bone (greater/lesser cornua, stylohyoid lig).
    • Inferior Constrictor: Attaches to thyroid/cricoid cartilages. Lowest horizontal part = Cricopharyngeus (acts as sphincter preventing air into esophagus. Failure to relax causes high pressure → Pharyngeal Pouch / Zenker's Diverticulum at Killian's dehiscence → dysphagia).
    • All insert posteriorly into the midline Pharyngeal Raphe (attaches to pharyngeal tubercle on basilar part of occipital bone).
    • Innervation: Pharyngeal plexus (Vagus N. motor fibers).
  • Structures Traversing Gaps Between Constrictors:
    • Above Superior: Auditory tube, Levator veli palatini, ascending palatine a.
    • Between Superior & Middle: Stylopharyngeus muscle, Glossopharyngeal N. (CN IX).
    • Between Middle & Inferior: Internal laryngeal nerve, Superior laryngeal artery (pierce thyrohyoid membrane).
    • Below Inferior: Recurrent laryngeal nerve, Inferior laryngeal artery.
  • Longitudinal Muscles: Stylopharyngeus (CN IX), Salpingopharyngeus (CN X), Palatopharyngeus (CN X). Elevate larynx/pharynx during swallowing.
  • Sensory Nerves: Nasopharynx (V2), Oropharynx (CN IX), Laryngopharynx (Internal Laryngeal N of Vagus).
  • Deglutition (Swallowing): 1) Voluntary: Styloglossus/palatoglossus push bolus back. 2) Involuntary: Soft palate elevates (shuts nasopharynx), Larynx elevated by longitudinal muscles, epiglottis caps inlet, constrictors push bolus down to esophagus.
HNSS-A-029: LARYNX ANATOMY, MUSCLES & NEUROVASCULATURE
  • Laryngeal Cartilages (9 total): Inlet to C6.
    • Single: Thyroid (largest, Adam's apple/laryngeal prominence, V angle, oblique line, sup/inf cornua), Cricoid (signet ring, completely circular), Epiglottis (elastic cartilage flap valve, attaches to thyroid).
    • Paired: Arytenoid (pyramid-shaped, vocal processes attach to vocal cords, muscular processes attach to muscles), Corniculate (articulate with arytenoid apices), Cuneiform (strengthen aryepiglottic folds).
    • Joints: Cricothyroid, Cricoarytenoid (synovial, multiaxial).
  • Ligaments & Membranes:
    • Thyrohyoid membrane: Connects thyroid to hyoid. Pierced by Int. Laryngeal N & Sup Laryngeal A.
    • Quadrangular membrane: Extends between epiglottis & arytenoid. Thickened inferior margin forms Vestibular Ligaments (False vocal cords/folds - fixed, pink, vascular).
    • Cricothyroid ligament: Interconnects cricoid, thyroid, arytenoid. Upper free margin forms Vocal Ligaments (True vocal cords/folds - mobile, white, avascular). Space between is Rima Glottidis.

HNSS-A-030: TONSILS & WALDEYER'S RING
  • Waldeyer's Ring: Ring of lymphoid tissue at the entrance of the respiratory & digestive tracts. Formed by Pharyngeal, Tubal, Palatine, and Lingual tonsils. Max size in childhood, atrophies after puberty.
  • Palatine Tonsils:
    • Location: In the oropharynx between the palatoglossal arch (anteriorly) and palatopharyngeal arch (posteriorly).
    • Anatomical Features: Medial free surface projects into pharynx, pitted by numerous small openings leading to tonsillar crypts. Lateral surface covered by fibrous tonsillar capsule. Separated from superior constrictor muscle (its bed) by loose areolar tissue.
    • Relations: Lateral to superior constrictor lies the styloglossus muscle, loop of the facial artery, and Internal Carotid Artery.
    • Vascular Supply: Tonsillar branch of Facial Artery. Veins pierce superior constrictor and join the External Palatine Vein. This vein descends from the soft palate and is a common source of severe postoperative bleeding after tonsillectomy!
    • Lymph Drainage: Jugulodigastric node (upper deep cervical node below/behind angle of mandible; usually enlarged/tender in tonsillitis).
    • Clinical: Quinsy (peritonsillar abscess) - caused by spread of infection from palatine tonsil to loose connective tissue outside capsule. Pus pools between tonsil and superior constrictor.
  • Pharyngeal Tonsil (Adenoids):
    • Location: Submucosa of the roof of the nasopharynx.
    • Clinical: Excessive hypertrophy due to infection = Adenoids. Blocks posterior nasal openings (choanae) → mouth breathing, loud snoring. Close relationship to auditory tube can block it, causing deafness & recurrent otitis media. Adenoidectomy is treatment. Viewed via posterior rhinoscopy (mirror passed through mouth).
  • Lingual Tonsil: Submucosa of the posterior 1/3 of the tongue (pharyngeal part). Devoid of papillae but has irregular nodular surface due to underlying lymph nodules.
HNSS-Ag-002: HEAD & NECK (AGE CHANGES OF MANDIBLE)
  • Neonatal Mandible:
    • Consists of two separate dentary bones joined by a midline symphyseal joint.
    • The two halves fuse at the symphysis menti by the end of the 1st year.
    • Angle of mandible is highly obtuse.
    • Head (condyloid process) is level with the upper margin of the body.
    • Coronoid process lies at a superior level to the head.
  • Adult Mandible:
    • Angle assumes adult shape (approaches 90°) after eruption of permanent teeth.
    • Head and neck grow so that the head (condyle) comes to lie higher than the coronoid process.
    • Alveolar process is prominent and contains 16 sockets for adult teeth.
  • Old Age Mandible:
    • Size reduces significantly when teeth are lost.
    • Alveolar part of the bone resorbs and becomes much smaller.
    • Ramus becomes oblique in position so that the head is bent posteriorly.
    • Angle becomes obtuse once again.
